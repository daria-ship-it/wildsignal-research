GEO YouTube Audit Framework

Field guide · Wild Signal Agency July 2026

The six-domain framework for auditing a YouTube channel's readiness to be found, cited, and referenced by AI systems.

When to use this framework

  • A client asks why their YouTube channel is not surfacing in AI answers
  • Before launching a YouTube-focused GEO or content program
  • As a periodic (quarterly) health check on an established channel
  • When pitching a YouTube-heavy brand and you need a credible diagnostic

Required Inputs

  1. Channel URL (e.g. youtube.com/@ExploreCanada)
  2. Channel operator / parent brand (the entity that runs the channel)
  3. Primary topics / queries the client wants to be cited for (3–10 high-intent queries)
  4. Athena export, if available — urls-export, sources_export, responses_export CSVs for the relevant period. Treated as required when available; the audit notes "cross-check not performed — no Athena data provided" only if the client genuinely has none.
  5. Competitor channels (1–3 optional benchmarks)

If any required input is missing, confirm it before the audit begins.

Audit Framework

Score each domain 1–5 (1 = critical gaps, 5 = fully optimized). The overall AI Visibility Score is the rounded average.

Domain 1 · Crawlability & Access

Whether AI systems can reach and parse channel content. Channel-level: not robots.txt, but YouTube-side and About-panel configurations.

Check What to look for
About-panel website link Resolves? Correct destination? No DNS failures?
Channel visibility Public, Unlisted, or Private? Any key videos set to Unlisted?
Featured video status Hero video on the Home tab — is it Public?
Social row consistency Each row label matches the actual destination (Pinterest row → pinterest.com, not twitter.com)
Country tag Is the channel's country tag set and visible?
Channel localization Are descriptions, titles, and About panel populated in primary market language(s)?

Domain 2 · Entity Signals

The chain of corroborating signals AI uses to confirm the channel as an entity: verification, brand consistency, knowledge graph, cross-platform identity.

Check What to look for
Channel verification Is the consumer channel verified by YouTube?
Cross-linking to sibling channels If the brand runs multiple channels (B2B, regional), do they featured-channel each other?
Wikidata entity Does an accurate Wikidata entry exist? Are subsidiaries/parents declared correctly?
Wikipedia presence Does the brand have a Wikipedia article? Does it reference the channel?
Google Knowledge Panel Does the brand have one? Does it surface the YouTube channel?
Brand name consistency Channel name, About description, social handles all aligned?
sameAs / social profile graph All major social handles point to the channel and vice versa?

Domain 3 · Schema & Structured Data

YouTube auto-generates VideoObject schema per upload. The audit evaluates the supporting schema — channel-level metadata, the linked website, and the structured cues that wrap individual videos.

Check What to look for
VideoObject baseline Auto-generated per upload — note any anomalies (missing thumbnails, broken descriptions)
Transcript property Are transcripts attached to VideoObject schema (on the linked website where videos are embedded)?
Organization schema (linked site) Is the linked website's Organization schema declaring the YouTube channel as a sameAs?
FAQPage schema on Q&A-shaped content Itinerary / how-to videos with description blocks structured as Q&A — wrapped in FAQPage on the linked site?
Channel-page metadata Title, description, keywords — using channel defaults or enhanced?
Closed captions / SDH Are CC tracks present on top videos? Auto-generated or human-edited?

Domain 4 · Content Architecture

The catalog's structure. AI engines parse playlists, sections, and series as topic clusters.

Check What to look for
Playlist organization Are playlists grouped logically (by topic, season, region, theme)?
Pillar / series content Are there recurring series with a clear template (e.g. "X Day Itinerary")?
Sections on the Home tab Are sections curated, or showing default uploads?
Cross-link patterns Do videos link to related videos in descriptions and end screens?
Shorts ↔ long-form bridges Does Shorts content link to or reference the parent long-form videos?
Topical depth Does the catalog cover sub-topics thoroughly within each pillar?

Domain 5 · Content Quality

Per-video standards. Evaluate a sample of 5–10 representative videos: top-performing, most recent, and one per major playlist.

Check What to look for
Title pattern Query-matching vs. evocative-only. Does the title include the high-intent keyword?
Description depth Full paragraph summary, or one-liner? Are descriptions consistent in structure?
Description CTAs / sign-offs Recurring channel attribution, transcript link, related-video links?
Question-answering format For how-to or itinerary content, does the video lead with the direct answer in the first 15 seconds?
Entity-rich language Specific names, dates, places, numbers — or generic copy?
Series template adherence If the channel has a citable template (like X Day Itinerary), is it being applied consistently?

Domain 6 · Multimodal Assets

Thumbnails, on-screen text, captions, transcripts. YouTube is the #1 social source cited in AI Overviews — but only when the text layer around the video is rich.

Check What to look for
Thumbnail consistency Are thumbnails brand-aligned, on-screen-text-rich, visually scannable?
On-screen text in video Does the video include captioned highlights (place names, dates, key facts)?
Closed captions accuracy Sample 2–3 videos: are auto-captions accurate, or are there human-edited tracks?
Published transcripts Are transcripts available on the linked website, keyed to destinations / topics?
Chapter markers Are long-form videos chaptered with descriptive chapter titles?
Shorts alignment Do Shorts include voiceover + on-screen text + structured intro/outro?

The Athena Cross-Check (when data is available)

Athena exports give a multi-LLM citation reality check. The cross-check answers one question: does AI actually cite this channel today, and if not, where is it citing from instead?

Methodology:

  1. Match the full channel catalog (all video IDs and slugs) against every cited source URL in the Athena sources_export and urls-export files for the relevant period.
  2. Sanity-check the matching logic: pick 2–3 known strings (a place name, a unique Shorts ID) and confirm they return expected hits.
  3. Report three numbers: YouTube's domain rank in the corpus, total YouTube responses influenced, and the share of those that come from the client's channel.
  4. If the share is zero or near-zero while YouTube ranks in the top 5 domains, lead the report with that finding — it makes the "metadata problem, not a content problem" case concretely.

If the client has no Athena data, run the audit without it and add one line in the executive summary: "Cross-check against the Athena multi-LLM dataset was not performed for this engagement."

Voice and Editorial Standards

Audits use the Wild Signal AP-30% house voice: declarative confidence, specificity over volume, short punch then expand. Apply these rules:

  • Em dashes: maximum 1 per paragraph, maximum 3 per page. Most paragraphs should have zero.
  • Banned words / phrases: leverage, optimize, unlock, curate, critically, comprehensive, robust, holistic, ecosystem, fundamentally, navigate, elevate, harness, foster, empower, deep dive, closing the gap, closing this gap, at the end of the day, in today's landscape.
  • False pivots ("not X, it's Y"): maximum 1 per document.
  • Bold accents: maximum 3–5 bolded words per paragraph. Reserve bold-italic for 4–6 keystone moments per report (e.g. "Exactly the depth AI engines weight as authority."). Place them where the eye should land.
  • AI-voice tells: avoid hedged phrases like "Whether you are…", "It's important to note that…", "In summary…", "Let's explore…".
  • Numbers: be concrete. "169M lifetime views" beats "millions of views." Always include the unit.
  • Acknowledge LLMs broadly, not by brand name, in the cover and exec summary. Specific platforms (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity) are fine in the body when relevant to a finding.

Key Data Points (Reference)

Use these to support findings:

  • YouTube is the #1 social source cited in AI Overviews
  • Transcripts — not the audio or video — are what AI reads and cites
  • Pages with VideoObject + transcript property see 317% citation lift vs. text-only
  • 96% of AI Overview citations from sources with strong E-E-A-T
  • 85% of AIO citations from content published in last 2 years
  • Verified channels carry weight as a basic credibility signal
  • Entity-rich text cited at 3x rate of generic text
  • Pages with 15+ recognized entities show 4.8x higher selection probability
  • Only 18% of brands are visible across all three major AI platforms
  • AI-referred traffic has grown 527% year-over-year
